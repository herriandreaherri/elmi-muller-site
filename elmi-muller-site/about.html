Her story

A surgeon who broke the rules to save lives — and never stopped.

Elmi Muller is a South African transplant surgeon and Dean of the Faculty of Medicine and Health Sciences at Stellenbosch University. Her path to the stage began not in a green room, but in an operating theatre — and in a decision most of her field considered unthinkable.

In 2008, at Groote Schuur Hospital in Cape Town, Elmi pioneered the world's first programme to transplant kidneys from HIV-positive donors into HIV-positive recipients. At the time, transplant guidelines and conventional wisdom said it couldn't — or shouldn't — be done. She built the programme anyway, working through the ethical, clinical and institutional resistance it raised. It went on to save thousands of lives and helped reshape transplant guidelines internationally.

She is past president of the Southern African Transplantation Society and currently serves as vice-president of The Transplantation Society (TTS), connecting her with transplant leaders worldwide. Her work has been recognised in The Lancet and with the Shoprite Checkers Woman of the Year award, and she continues to lead both clinically and academically as Dean at Stellenbosch University.
